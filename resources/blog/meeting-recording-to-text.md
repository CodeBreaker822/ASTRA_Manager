A meeting transcript and meeting minutes are related, but they are not the same document. The transcript is a detailed written draft of what was said. Minutes are a shorter, reviewed account of decisions, actions, and relevant discussion.

An audio-to-text tool can speed up both tasks when you preserve the source, review important wording, and keep the different versions clearly labeled.

## Before the meeting: prepare for a better transcript

Choose a recording device that can hear the whole room. Place it on a stable surface near the speakers rather than beside a fan, projector, keyboard, or open window.

If the meeting is remote, use the platform's approved recording method and confirm which audio tracks it captures. Follow your organization's notice, consent, retention, and access rules.

Prepare a short reference list containing:

- Participant names and roles
- Project or office names
- Agenda items
- Acronyms and technical terms
- Expected dates or reference numbers

This list is valuable during review because speech recognition often produces a plausible but incorrect spelling for unfamiliar names.

## Record with review in mind

Start with the meeting title and date when appropriate. Ask participants to speak one at a time and identify themselves when the room or format makes voices difficult to distinguish.

When a decision is made, the chair or facilitator can restate it clearly. When an action is assigned, repeat the owner and due date. This improves the meeting record even before transcription.

Do not rely on speaker diarization to solve heavy overlap. Automated grouping can separate some detected voices, but it does not know each participant's identity and can be wrong when voices are similar or simultaneous.

## Review the meeting transcript

Review in chronological order and use source playback whenever the generated text is uncertain.

Prioritize:

1. Motions, approvals, and final decisions
2. Action items, owners, and deadlines
3. Financial amounts, dates, and reference numbers
4. Commitments or objections attributed to a person
5. Direct quotations that will appear in a report
6. Names of participants, organizations, and projects

Check that the transcript begins and ends at the expected points. Resolve missing or duplicated sections before polishing. Keep the raw result available so later edits can be compared with the speech-recognition output.

## Create minutes from the transcript

A practical minutes structure is:

### Meeting details

- Title
- Date and time
- Location or platform
- Chair or facilitator
- Attendees and absences

### Agenda discussion

Summarize the material needed to understand each outcome. Do not copy every conversational turn unless the required record calls for it.

### Decisions

Write each final decision clearly. If approval was conditional, include the condition.

### Action items

Record the task, responsible person or group, and due date. Replay the relevant source section when any element is uncertain.

### Follow-up

Include the next meeting or review point when it was actually agreed.

JERVA can generate an online summary, but treat that output as a draft. A summary model may omit a condition, combine separate actions, or assign a statement to the wrong context. Compare it with the reviewed transcript and audio.

## Transcript, clean transcript, summary, and minutes

Use distinct labels to prevent confusion:

- **Raw transcript:** the direct automatic speech-recognition result
- **Clean transcript:** text corrected and formatted after review
- **Summary:** a shortened description generated or written from the transcript
- **Approved minutes:** the organization's final meeting record after its required review process

Automatic transcription does not turn a recording into approved minutes by itself.

## Export and store the result

TXT is useful for a simple archive or moving text into another tool. A Word-compatible export is useful for editing minutes and reports. An Excel-compatible export can preserve transcript sections and structured fields for detailed review.

Control access to both audio and text. A transcript is searchable and easy to copy, which can make it more sensitive than the original recording in some workflows. Apply the appropriate retention period rather than keeping every source indefinitely.

## Meeting transcription checklist

- Recording and transcription were permitted.
- The microphone captured all participants clearly enough.
- The full meeting appears in the transcript.
- Decisions, actions, names, dates, and amounts were replayed.
- Speaker labels were verified before names were assigned.
- Raw transcript, clean transcript, summary, and minutes are not confused.
- The final files are stored with suitable access controls.
